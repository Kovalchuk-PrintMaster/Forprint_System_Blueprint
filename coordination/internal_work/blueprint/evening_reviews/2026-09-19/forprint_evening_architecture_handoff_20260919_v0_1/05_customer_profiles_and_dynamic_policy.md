# Customer Profiles and Dynamic Policy

## Principle

Do not treat customer profiles as permanent labels.

Managers need a simple entry UX, while the internal model may be multidimensional.

## Seed profile UX

A manager should be able to create a customer and choose one of a small number of familiar presets/buttons.

The preset initializes policy defaults.

It is not a permanent classification.

## Three logical layers

### Seed Profile
Human-selected initial policy.

### Observed Customer Model
Evidence accumulated from actual behavior.

### Effective Policy
The currently applicable operational rules.

## Separate three kinds of data

### Descriptive
What is observed.

### Preferences
What the customer usually chooses.

### Control Policy
What ForPrint allows.

A descriptive observation must not silently grant more operational authority.

## Review mechanics

Profile review should be both:
- event driven;
- periodic/reconciliation based.

Possible triggers:
- enough new confirmed orders;
- repeated correction/dispute;
- payment pattern shift;
- preference drift;
- new product family;
- maturity threshold crossed;
- machine model diverges from seed profile.

## Dynamic customer movement

Track dimensions such as:
- payment health;
- commercial value;
- growth momentum;
- margin quality;
- service burden;
- order clarity;
- dispute risk;
- relationship stability;
- strategic potential.

Track trajectory, not only current value.

## Hysteresis

Single events should generally not trigger radical reclassification.

## Company-side state

Effective customer policy must also be able to depend on current ForPrint conditions:
- available working capital;
- current credit exposure;
- production load;
- desired margin;
- strategic priorities.

## Manager attention

Prefer exception/movement reports instead of manual review of every customer.

## Audit

Preserve profile transitions and reasons.
Do not overwrite history.
